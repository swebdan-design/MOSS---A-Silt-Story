# MOSS — game design

## Intent

A short narrative adventure in which progression is learning to listen. The
boy does not gain levels, items or combat skills; he gains *ways of
listening*, and each becomes a behaviour of a real generative instrument. The
player begins thinking they are learning to make the pond's music and ends
having learned to take part in something that was already there.

At first the boy hears almost nothing (the first-day world has only sparse
pebble notes). Then individual sounds (each discovery adds a layer, in the
world and at the pond). Then relationships (the bell's interval in the chime
and in the pond; insects that leave room for the melody). Then he makes music
(the pond instrument). Then he listens without intervening (Stillness, the
SELECT listening mode). Finally he leaves, and the pond continues.

## Structure

| Day | Act | Palette | Goals (all optional exploration aside) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Arrival | I | afternoon → dusk | meet Mae; find the pond; drop the first pebble; get the notebook |
| 2 Listening | II | bright | HOLLOW WOOD (log, with Iris), WIND (reeds), WATER (Three Stones), INSECTS (hill meadow) |
| 3 Rain | III | rain | RAIN (shelter), ECHO (Last Bell), the Hidden Path, STILLNESS (clearing) |
| 4 The Last Afternoon | IV | golden → dusk | MEMORY (Mae's wind chime), the Pond's Answer, a last evening |
| 5 Departure | V | dawn | the final visit: the stone, the leaving, the pond alone |

A day ends when the boy sleeps; the bed explains gently if something is still
waiting. Days never depend on real time.

## The Sound Notebook

Opened with SELECT or from the menu. Page one says the day, a hint for where
to go next and how many ways of listening have been found. Pages two to nine
are the discoveries in the order the player found them, each with an
original 32 × 32 illustration, the place, the observation in the boy's words
and what it changes at the pond. Undiscovered pages are blank. After the
ending a last page appears.

## Listening

Standing still for a moment (Quick ≈ 1.3 s, Patient ≈ 2.5 s, chosen in
Settings) turns on the listening state: a ring icon over the boy, the melody
yields, the ambience stays. In a listening zone, about a second later, the
place reveals something. Outside zones nothing is forced on the player.

Every audio clue has a visual and textual twin, so the game is playable
without sound: the creek's song is shown as ring sizes; the hidden path's
sound comes with notes drifting from the west and a line of text; the
crickets come with small lights; the stillness event has rising lights.

## Puzzles

| Puzzle | How it works | Other ways through | Failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Hidden Path | listen near the ferns in the wood: a faint sound from the west, notes drifting in; the ferns open | talk to the cat sitting by the ferns on day 3 | none |
| The Three Stones | the water upstream rings big, little, middle; the three stones are middle, low, high. Knock low, high, middle | read the rings (no sound needed); listen on the bridge for the words "deep… high… in between"; hints after 6 and 12 tries | none |
| The Rain Shelter | stand under the shelter in the rain and listen | Mr Hollis suggests it | none |
| The Last Bell | ring the bell in the rain; the cutting echoes it | Mr Vale invites you to | none |
| The Still Clearing | stand still in the middle | — | none |
| The Pond's Answer | at the pond on day 4, drop a pebble on the lowest row, then the second row — the bell's interval | the notebook's hint page says "Low, then high, like the bell" | none |

No puzzle needs musical theory or perfect pitch; nothing can be failed.

## People

- **Mae** — practical, warm; packing up a life without making a speech of it.
  She gives the notebook and the chime.
- **Iris** — energetic, imaginative, unsentimental. Shows the log and the
  stones. Will "look at" the pond after he leaves.
- **Mr Hollis** — the gardener. "Seasons don't end, lad. They hand over."
- **Ada** — the shop. Community, teapots, waving from windows.
- **Mr Vale** — the retired stationmaster who still comes up. "You hear a
  thing best by its gap."
- **The cat** — no dialogue, no explanation. Sometimes it sits where something is.
- **The reed creature** — glimpsed at the pond; answers the first pebble and
  the final gesture. Never explained.

## The ending

Day 5, dawn. The boy walks to the bank and sits. The pond plays the final
composition, built from his garden and the pond's memory. The notebook's last
page. He leaves a small stone on the bank, stands, and walks away. The view
stays on the empty bank and the water for about forty seconds; the reed
creature appears once. No input is possible. Then: "I thought I had found the
pond. / But it was always here." The pond keeps playing under the words and
under the ending card — about a minute and a half of music after the boy has
gone. The ending card (MOSS / A SILT Story / by Synth Centric) appears
over the still-playing pond, then the title, which now offers **Return to the
pond**. Progress is kept; Continue resumes the last afternoon for free
exploration.

## Accessibility

Text speed (Slow, Normal, Fast; A or B hurries any text), listening time
(Quick, Patient), Mono sound, master volume, and visual equivalents for every
sound clue.
